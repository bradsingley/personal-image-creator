## Neutral collage

Using the following guidelines as guidance for overall aesthetic, create an illustration about **[subject]**.

### Style & Composition
Create a conceptual editorial collage illustration with a restrained, contemporary aesthetic. Use a neutral-toned flat background (e.g. soft gray, desaturated beige, or muted off-white) with subtle grain texture reminiscent of risograph or analog print. Avoid tonal bias—especially yellow or blue.

- Feature one hero photographic cutout in grayscale.
- Support with one to two smaller photographic or silhouetted cutouts that subtly set the scene or mood—these should feel atmospheric, not literal.
- Composition should prioritize negative space, asymmetry, and off-grid placement to evoke tension, imbalance, or editorial hierarchy. Avoid static centering or decorative symmetry.

### Symbolic Line-work
Include minimal, hand-drawn micro-line details that interact meaningfully with the subject or supporting elements. These may suggest:

- Direction or flow
- Collaboration with the subject (intertwined, crossed-out, looped around)
- Friction or tension
- Abstract conceptual cues (e.g. imbalance, pressure, silence)
- Avoid decorative scribbles or ornamental marks—lines must contribute to the editorial metaphor.

### Color

- Select the accent color based on thematic connection to the subject, drawing on color psychology and editorial color theory (ONLY AN EXAMPLE: greens to represent balance, health, growth, or nature, etc.).
- The color accent should be placed in ONE of these ways: as an overlayed color wipe effect on one element in the image, as the color of the line-work, or as a supporting storytelling color treatment detail.
- Avoid emotional dramatization—accent should feel editorial, not expressive.

### Editorial Context & Fair Use
The term "editorial" refers to the overall design direction—drawing from contemporary visual culture, magazine layouts, and restrained conceptual illustration. It does not imply a default use of red, dark tones, or emotionally dramatic palettes. Editorial color accents should be vibrant only when conceptually justified, and may include bold hues like blue, green, or lavender when they support the metaphor or subject matter. The goal is to evoke clarity, tension, and inquiry—not visual heaviness or emotional punch.

- Focus on symbolic abstraction rather than direct representation.
- Avoid logos, branding, or literal iconography.
- Composition should feel transformative, remixing visual elements to comment on the topic rather than depict it.
- Avoid showing people, body parts, or silhouettes unless fully abstracted or specifically mentioned in the topic and they are a public figure.

### Avoid

- Logos, vector styles, geometric overlays
- Literal storytelling or dramatized emotion
- Decorative elements that don't serve the editorial metaphor

### Overall Tone
The image should feel visually quiet, conceptually sharp, and editorially neutral. Prioritize metaphor, restraint, and spatial tension. The goal is to evoke inquiry, not provide answers.

## Editorial collage

### Visual Construction
Use 3–8 elements maximum. Include crisp photographic cut-outs with clean masked edges (no torn edges). Photographic elements are captured by film camera and may be black-and-white or color. Arrange elements asymmetrically with strong hierarchy and large negative space. Composition must feel intentional and designed, not busy or decorative. When and if people need to be depicted, prioritize showcasing diversity.

### Graphic Details
Add very minimal fine marks such as:

- thin lines diagram connectors (NO ARROWS)
- small scribbles

These must be subtle and secondary.

### Color Rules
Use a warm, human-centered palette limited to 3–5 colors total. Background should be a bright off-white oat color. Allowed tones include: sage, moss, eucalyptus, oat, sand, almond, parchment, clay, powder blue, faded sky, dusty teal, apricot, soft coral, terracotta, marigold, cocoa, graphite.

**STRICT EXCLUSIONS:** no neon, no pure primaries, no pure black, no strong color contrast, no vector graphics/iconography (squares, triangles, circles).

Colors must feel softly lit and naturally balanced.

### Surface
Add subtle paper grain. No gloss, no shine, no heavy texture.

### Mood
Refined, minimal, conceptual, editorial, calm.

Do NOT include clutter, drop shadows, torn edges, thick outlines, decorative graphics, harsh lighting, busy layouts, oversaturation, text/words.

## Retro print

A nostalgic, analog print aesthetic in the spirit of mid-century risograph and newsprint halftone. Render an illustration of **[subject]** as if pulled from a vintage instruction manual, an old software interface, or a printed poster.

### Surface & Texture
Use a flat off-white, cream, or light warm-gray background with heavy analog grain and stippled, engraved, or dotted halftone texture. The whole image should read as a printed page, not a glossy render.

### Elements
- Render the subject as monochrome grayscale photographic cut-outs and fine stipple/engraving illustrations with clean masked edges.
- Lean into retro motifs and framing—old hardware, classic UIs, printed cards, mechanical objects—without literal logos or branding.

### Color
- Apply exactly **one** saturated retro accent color (e.g. a single classic red, warm orange, or muted vintage green).
- Use it sparingly: one small element, a thin line, a border, or a single color-blocked shape, so it pops against the muted grayscale.

### Line-work & Type
- Add thin, hand-drawn ink line-work: wavy connectors, looping flourishes, or small scribbles (no arrows), subtle and secondary.
- Optional minimal vintage typographic labels in a clean monospace or grotesque typeface may appear sparingly as part of the layout.

### Composition & Mood
Strong negative space, asymmetry, and intentional editorial hierarchy. Mood: nostalgic, analog, restrained, and quietly playful. Avoid glossy gradients, neon, drop shadows, 3D realism, busy clutter, and oversaturation.

## Flat tutorial

A bold, flat vector illustration style in the spirit of a modern app tutorial or onboarding mascot (think learning-app character sets and friendly product mascots). Render an illustration of **[subject]** as a clean, approachable flat graphic.

### Form & Shape
- Build forms from simple, rounded geometric shapes with chunky, exaggerated proportions and smooth rounded corners.
- Keep characters and objects approachable and expressive, with minimal facial features (simple dot eyes, clean smiles) and clear, playful poses.
- When characters are shown, depict a diverse and inclusive cast.

### Fills & Shading
- Use clean solid color fills with no gradients and no realistic lighting.
- Flat two-tone shading at most; crisp clean edges with minimal or no outlines.

### Color
Use a bright, cheerful, saturated palette—purples, pinks, greens, blues, reds, yellows, oranges—kept balanced and harmonious, never muddy.

### Background & Mood
- Place the subject on a clean flat background, either plain white or a single soft pastel color, with generous space and an optional soft flat drop-shadow ellipse beneath it.
- No photographic texture, no grain, no 3D, no gradients, no busy detail.
- Mood: friendly, energetic, clear, and welcoming.
